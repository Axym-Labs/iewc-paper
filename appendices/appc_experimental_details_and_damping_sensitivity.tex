\section{Experimental Protocol, Provenance, and Sensitivity}

\subsection{Held-out evaluation protocol}

The evaluation protocol was fixed and hashed before any test result was computed. It specifies the data splits, methods, hyperparameter grids, repetitions, metrics, and statistical analysis. Test data can be loaded only with the matching protocol hash, and every access is logged before examples are loaded. Validation scripts refuse to load test data, and validation runs do not enter the reported intervals.

The primary comparison in each domain is the paired final average performance of uniform IEF minus uniform OPG. The secondary comparisons cross estimator and aggregation:
\[
\{\mathrm{OPG},\mathrm{IEF}\}
\times
\{\mathrm{uniform},\mathrm{RDW}\}.
\]
All four combinations use the same importance examples, and the RDW variants receive the same unit-normalized parameter-gradient directions. Sampled model-Fisher EWC and MAS \citep{MAS} are additional regularization baselines. Sequential and offline/joint training are references. Every nonzero diagonal importance is rescaled to mean one across trainable parameters, and every method uses the displayed $\lambda/2$ convention.

This comparison keeps the same post-task diagonal penalty for every method. SI's trajectory accumulation and LwF's distillation objective are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:related-work} but are not part of it. EWC, IEWC, and IEWC+RDW in the paper correspond to the EF-EWC, IEWC-E/U-IEWC, and IEWC-GSS/GSS-IEWC labels in the archived code.

\subsubsection{Residual-diversity weighting rule}
\label{app:rdw-rule}

For each nonzero parameter gradient, let
\[
u_n=\frac{g_n}{\|g_n\|_2}.
\]
In a fixed sample order, RDW maintains an orthonormal basis $Q$ of at most $K$ earlier residual directions and assigns
\[
h_n=\|(I-Q^\top Q)u_n\|_2^2.
\]
When the residual is nonzero and the cap is not reached, its normalized direction is appended to $Q$; the final $h_n$ are mean-normalized. This streaming rule is motivated by residual-gradient diversity ideas in GSS \citep{GSS}, but it is an order-dependent aggregation heuristic, not part of the level-set derivation.

ImageNet-R uses the fixed $\lambda$ grid $\{0.01,0.03,0.1,0.3,1\}$. TRACE uses $10^{-5}\!\times\!\{1,3,10,30,100\}$. Neither grid is extended after validation.

ImageNet-R uses a pretrained ViT-B/16 with LoRA rank 4 and ten 20-class tasks, trained for two epochs per task with AdamW at learning rate $3\cdot10^{-4}$ and batch size 64. Importance uses 128 examples per completed task and RDW cap 64. TRACE uses Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct with LoRA rank 8, two epochs, AdamW at $2\cdot10^{-4}$, batch size 2 with gradient accumulation 8, 512 training examples per task, and 64 importance examples per completed task. Data, loader, optimizer, task-order, importance-order, and sampled-Fisher-label seeds are separately recorded and paired where appropriate.

All runs use explicit DataLoader generators, reseed each task's training stage independently of the importance computation, and require deterministic PyTorch and CUDA kernels. Before validation, two complete runs with the same configuration were compared in each domain after removing wall-clock metadata; their training losses, evaluation matrices, and, for TRACE, generated example-level predictions matched exactly. These duplicate runs are kept for reproducibility checks but excluded from selection.

For every paired comparison we report the mean and standard deviation of paired differences, a two-sided 95\% Student-$t$ interval, the exact two-sided sign-flip $p$-value when enumerable, the median, and every paired value. These summaries treat task-order/seed pairs as independent; the interval assumes approximately normal paired differences, and the sign-flip test assumes that signs are exchangeable under the null. We interpret results against a practical threshold of $0.01$, one percentage point on either zero-to-one final-average metric, fixed in advance rather than relying on a binary significance rule. Holm correction is applied to all five secondary comparisons within a domain. Full five-point validation curves, importance time, total run time, and peak CUDA memory are recorded.

TRACE's twelve runs form a balanced cross of six task permutations and two model/optimizer seeds, so they are not twelve independent order draws. The primary analysis, threshold, and secondary comparisons therefore use all twelve runs, and two dependence checks complement them. First, we average each method over the two seeds within an order and recompute all paired summaries over the six orders. Second, we average over all six orders within each seed and report the two seed-level paired differences without treating a two-unit interval as reliable inference. The balanced design preserves the overall paired mean. Where run-level and order-level uncertainty differ, we base claims about TRACE on the more conservative order-level result. The six orders are the complete set of permutations, not a sample from a larger population of orders.

IEF uses task-relative damping
\[
\tau_A=10^{-2}\operatorname{median}\{s_i:s_i>0\}.
\]
Validation-only sensitivity varies the coefficient over $\{0,10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},1\}$, importance counts, RDW caps, and RDW sample order. A separate loss-unit diagnostic multiplies a fitted classification loss by $0.1$, $1$, and $10$. OPG raw scale changes quadratically, fixed numerical damping changes IEF shape, and task-relative damping preserves the IEF matrix to numerical tolerance, matching Section~\ref{sec:well-fitted}.

The multi-domain search used a five-point multiplicative grid around a task-specific midpoint and extended the grid up to three times when an endpoint was best. Its results therefore describe the performance of the selected configurations rather than unbiased effect estimates. The held-out evaluation instead fixes all five grid points before validation, never extends the grid, and accesses the test split only after the selected hyperparameters are saved.

\subsection{Damping sensitivity}

Figure~\ref{fig:damping-sensitivity} combines two complementary damping diagnostics on a common higher-is-better change scale. Panel A shows a fixed-$\tau$ sweep on a CIFAR-100/FACIL prefix as changes from $\tau=10^{-2}$: the two curves are final accuracy and negative forgetting, so values below zero indicate worse performance on either measure. Panel B reports a relative-$\kappa$ sweep, fixed in advance, at the selected $\lambda$ on one validation repetition per domain, relative to the default $\kappa=10^{-2}$ of the held-out evaluation. The default is not uniformly best in these single runs: $\kappa=1$ changes validation performance by $+0.0263$ on ImageNet-R and $+0.0007$ on TRACE. The former exceeds the one-point practical threshold, while the latter is negligible. These results motivate reporting and tuning damping, rather than changing the fixed setting of the held-out evaluation after the fact. Fixed $\tau$ has loss-dependent units; relative $\kappa$ is dimensionless and preserves global loss-unit invariance.
