\section{Label-Noise Stress Test}

The original CIFAR-100 label-noise experiment provides a downstream stress test complementary to the direct matrix analysis in Appendix B. Its endpoints were available during method development, so the table reports descriptive selected performance rather than a selection-independent effect estimate. Uniform training-label flips create atypical high-loss samples, and the clean task-aware test set measures downstream retention. Across this stress test, IEWC loses less accuracy than observed-label OPG-EWC as the flip rate increases.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Method & $p=0$ & $p=0.1$ & $p=0.25$ \\
\midrule
OPG-EWC & 0.5617 $\pm$ 0.0062 & 0.5132 $\pm$ 0.0112 & 0.2773 $\pm$ 0.0320 \\
EWC-DR & 0.5932 $\pm$ 0.0296 & 0.5310 $\pm$ 0.0226 & 0.4598 $\pm$ 0.0095 \\
IEWC & 0.5978 $\pm$ 0.0260 & 0.5642 $\pm$ 0.0215 & 0.5322 $\pm$ 0.0239 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The original study also compared sorted diagonal entries on a CIFAR-100 prefix. That diagnostic does not identify clean and corrupt sample contributions and cannot establish the spectral mechanism by itself; Appendix B now supplies the direct per-sample full-matrix test. The diagonal summary remains useful as a complementary downstream view. It uses all 512 exported entries per matrix and reports the stored-tail mass ratio, normalized stored-tail profile change, and log-scale RMSE of the sorted entries.
