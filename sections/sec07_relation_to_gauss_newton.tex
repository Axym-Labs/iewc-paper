\section{Relation to Gauss-Newton}

Consider the scalar least-squares setting in Euclidean geometry. Suppose the model output is scalar, such that
\[
\ell_n(z)=\frac12(z-y_n)^2.
\]
Then
\[
r_n=z_n^A-y_n
\]
is scalar, and for $r_n\neq 0$ we have
\[
\frac{r_n^2}{r_n^2}=1.
\]
Therefore the \emph{undamped} Euclidean IEF contribution reduces to
\[
J_n^\top J_n,
\]
and averaging gives
\[
M_{\mathrm{IEF}}
=
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^N J_n^\top J_n.
\]
This is exactly the Gauss--Newton or output-Jacobian form for squared-error regression \citep{GaussNewton,MartensHF,MartensNG}, the standard practical preconditioning matrix for second-order optimization. This matches our geometric intuition: loss preservation is governed by how parameters move the output, which is encoded by the Jacobian $J$, not by the current output-gradient magnitude. This equivalence was made explicit in \citep{IEF}. With positive damping, the contribution is instead multiplied by $r_n^2/(r_n^2+\tau)$ and tends to zero at exact interpolation; the exact Gauss--Newton identity therefore requires $\tau=0$ and $r_n\ne0$.
