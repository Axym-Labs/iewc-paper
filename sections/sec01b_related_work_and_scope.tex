\section{Related Work and Scope}

EWC stores a quadratic approximation around earlier parameters and weights parameter motion by a Fisher-derived importance estimate \citep{EWC}. Subsequent work has clarified how multiple task penalties should be accumulated and placed EWC within broader Bayesian and regularization-based views of continual learning \citep{HuszarEWC,ProgressCompress,VCL}. A practical ambiguity remains: implementations called ``Fisher'' may use the model-label expectation, sampled model labels, observed labels, or minibatch aggregates. These estimators are not interchangeable in theory or practice \citep{KunstnerEF,VanDeVenFisher2025}. Our scalar-loss derivation concerns the observed-label OPG object; sampled model-Fisher EWC is evaluated separately rather than treated as another name for the same matrix.

Other regularization methods protect different objects. SI estimates parameter importance from the optimization trajectory, MAS uses sensitivity of the learned output function, and LwF distills old predictions on later inputs \citep{SI,MAS,LwF}. Functional regularization can instead preserve selected predictive functions or memorable points \citep{FunctionalGP,FROMP}. CLOC is complementary to this taxonomy: it begins by declaring a preservation object and constraint set, then asks which local stored penalty follows. The present paper studies scalar-loss preservation and loss-level-set-normal output preservation; it does not claim that these are the only useful choices or that replay, distillation, and parameter isolation share the same resource assumptions.

iEF was introduced as a normalized observed-gradient approximation for natural-gradient optimization \citep{IEF}. We recover its Euclidean algebra from a different question: the metric distance of an old output displacement from the tangent to its old loss level set. This provides a preservation interpretation but does not convert iEF into exact global curvature. Our additions relative to simply substituting iEF into EWC are the CLOC derivation, the matched output-metric generalization, the finite-damping and interpolation boundary, and the separation of normalization from aggregation weights. The RDW ablation borrows a streaming residual-diversity intuition from GSS \citep{GSS}; it is an explicitly order-dependent heuristic, not part of the level-set result.
