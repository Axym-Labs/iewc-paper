\section{Improved Elastic Weight Consolidation}

After the theoretical analysis of $M_{\mathrm{IEF}}$, we turn to the remaining design choices needed for an implementation. First, in Section 10 we already identified the singular case $r_n=0$ and introduced damping with parameter $\tau$ as the practical remedy. Second, using a full matrix is usually prohibitively expensive in memory and computation, so an approximation is necessary. \citep{EWC} suggested a diagonal approximation, which we adopt, while noting the empirical evidence from diffusion continual learning that indicates that diagonal Fisher approximations can underperform in diffusion settings \citep{Rank1Diffusion}. In addition, the contamination model of Appendix B suggests that the leading eigenspace of $M_{\mathrm{IEF}}$ can be more stable than that of $F_{\mathrm{EF}}$ in settings with atypical samples or samples that the model did not learn. Section 8 identified the empirical Fisher's implicit sample-weighting profile. Since alternative aggregation weights can be useful in some continual-learning settings, we include an explicit weighting function; possible weighting mechanisms appear in FROMP and GSS \citep{FROMP,GSS}. Therefore, let
\[
h:\mathcal D_A\times\Theta\to\mathbb R_{\ge 0},
\qquad
a\mapsto h(a,\theta),
\]
be the weighting function that assigns each sample its corresponding sample importance coefficient. To keep $\lambda$ as the global regularization strength, normalize the coefficients as
\[
\bar h(a_i,\theta)
=
\frac{h(a_i,\theta)}{\frac{1}{N_A}\sum_{j=1}^{N_A} h(a_j,\theta)},
\]
In P-CLOC, this is simply absorbed into the penalty function. We recover the IEF from \citep{IEF} with
\[
\bar h(a_i,\theta)=1,
\]
The empirical Fisher corresponds instead to the unnormalized weighting profile
\[
h(a_i,\theta)=w_i^{\,G}=r_i^\top G_i^{-1}r_i
\]
when we use $\tau=0$. With mean-normalized $\bar h$, this only differs by a global scale that can be absorbed into $\lambda$. This extension yields the final form of the importance matrix that we use:
\[
M_{\mathrm{IEF},G}  
=  
\frac1N  
\sum_{n=1}^N  
\bar h(a_n, \theta)
\frac{g_n g_n^\top}{r_n^\top G_n^{-1}r_n+\tau},  
\qquad  
g_n=J_n^\top r_n.  
\]
The free parameters of an implementation can be described more precisely as the four-tuple
\[
(\lambda,\tau,\Gamma,h).
\]
Here $\lambda$ is the strength of the preservation constraint from CLOC, $\tau$ controls damping for small-gradient samples, $h$ maps samples to their importance coefficient and $\Gamma$ specifies the output geometry. Concretely, we treat $G_n$ as the samplewise metric induced by the chosen geometry model $\Gamma$ at the old output. $\Gamma$ maps an output to the positive-definite matrix that constitutes the local quadratic approximation of the distance:
\[
\Gamma:\mathcal Z\to \mathbb S_{++}^{m},
\qquad
G_n=\Gamma\!\big(f_{\theta_A^\star}(a_n)\big).
\]
As motivated earlier, the canonical choice is to take $\Gamma(\cdot)=I$, $\tau \approx 0$ and $h(\cdot, \cdot)=1$. Empirically, $\tau=10^{-2}$ is a practical default for the diagonal CIFAR-100 runs, and Appendix C shows that different values measurably affect forgetting. Conversely, the remaining parameter is then $\lambda$ that intuitively controls the strength of the solution preservation constraint. For low-rank approximations of $M_{\mathrm{IEF}}$, one also has to determine the rank $k$, turning this into a five-tuple. \textbf{Together, these derivations and implementation choices define an implementable mechanism derived from PL-CLOC: EWC arises from scalar loss preservation, and IEWC extends this mechanism through normalization, damping, sample weighting, and output-geometry selection. We call this mechanism Improved Elastic Weight Consolidation (IEWC)}.
