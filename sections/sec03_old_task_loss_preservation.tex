\subsection{Scalar Functional Preservation}
\label{sec:scalar-functional-preservation}

Let $s_a:\Theta\to\mathbb R$ be a differentiable scalar functional of the model on old sample $a$. Preserving that functional means
\[
q(a,\theta)
=
s_a(\theta)-s_a(\theta_A^\star).
\]
Since preservation means $q(a,\theta)=0$, take $\mathcal C=\{0\}$. The corresponding unscaled proximal loss is
\[
p(a,\theta)
=
\operatorname{dist}(q(a,\theta),\mathcal C)^2
=
q(a,\theta)^2.
\]
To obtain a low-order Taylor approximation, use the perturbation around the old solution $\delta=\theta-\theta_A^\star$ in the first-order expansion of $q$:
\[
q(a,\theta_A^\star+\delta)
\approx
g_a^\top\delta,
\]
where
\[
g_a
=
\nabla_\theta s_a(\theta)\big|_{\theta=\theta_A^\star}.
\]

When the functional factors through the model output as $s_a(\theta)=\phi_a(f_\theta(x))$, write
\[
\begin{aligned}
z_{A,i} &= f_{\theta_A^\star}(x_i),\\
r_i &= \nabla_z \phi_{a_i}(z)\big|_{z=z_{A,i}},\\
J_i &= \nabla_\theta f_{\theta_A^\star}(x_i),
\end{aligned}
\]
the chain rule gives
\[
g_i
=
J_i^\top r_i.
\]

Substituting this expansion into the preservation penalty gives
\[
\frac{\lambda}{2N_A}\sum_{i=1}^{N_A} q(a_i,\theta)^2
\;\approx\;
\frac{\lambda}{2N_A}\sum_{i=1}^{N_A} (g_i^\top\delta)^2.
\]
Collecting the gradient outer products yields
\[
\frac{\lambda}{2N_A}\sum_{i=1}^{N_A} (g_i^\top\delta)^2
=
\frac{\lambda}{2}\,
\delta^\top
\left(
\frac{1}{N_A}\sum_{i=1}^{N_A} g_i g_i^\top
\right)
\delta
=
\frac{\lambda}{2}\,
\delta^\top
M_1
\delta.
\]

Therefore the resulting matrix is
\[
M_1
=
\frac{1}{N_A}\sum_{i=1}^{N_A} g_i g_i^\top
=
\frac{1}{N_A}\sum_{i=1}^{N_A} J_i^\top r_i r_i^\top J_i.
\]
This matrix encodes the local parameter directions in which the chosen scalar functional changes. The displayed quadratic is exact for the squared first-order surrogate and approximates the original nonlinear equality constraint at $\theta_A^\star$. The rest of the paper takes $\phi_a=\ell_A$ unless stated otherwise. This loss specialization is the \emph{outer-product loss-gradient} (OPG) matrix, and for negative log likelihood it becomes the empirical-Fisher algebra used by EWC, as specified next.
