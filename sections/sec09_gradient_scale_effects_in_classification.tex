\section{Gradient-Scale Effects in Classification}

The output-gradient weighting in observed-label OPG has an important classification symptom. For cross-entropy with softmax probabilities $p_n$ and one-hot label $y_n$, the output-loss gradient with respect to logits is
\[
r_n=p_n-y_n.
\]
For a confidently correct prediction with class $c$ and small $\varepsilon$,
\[
p_{n,c}=1-\varepsilon,
\qquad
\|r_n\|_2^2\in\Theta(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Thus the OPG contribution becomes small as the model becomes more confident and correct. This is coherent with its derivation: the scalar first-order loss change is also small. For continual-learning preservation, however, small current loss need not mean that losing the sample later is harmless. EWC-DR identifies this classification symptom and proposes a logit-reversal repair \citep{EWC-DR}.

In the present framework, that observation is one instance of the weighting effect identified in Section~8: observed-label OPG importance depends on output-gradient scale. Undamped IEF removes this particular factor. Positive damping retains the removal only above its transition scale, and empirical benefit remains a question for the controlled comparisons below.
