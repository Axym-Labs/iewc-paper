\section{Output-Space Geometry}

The scalar CLOC construction and its OPG matrix require no output-space metric. The level-set construction does: for a symmetric positive-definite primal metric $G_n$, output displacements have inner product $\langle u,v\rangle_{G_n}=u^\top G_n v$, the metric-normal direction is $G_n^{-1}r_n$, and the normal-distance denominator is $r_n^\top G_n^{-1}r_n$. Thus a geometry choice must supply a matched pair: $G_n$ acts on primal output displacement and $G_n^{-1}$ on the loss covector. An arbitrary denominator does not constitute a new geometry.

The canonical choice $G_n=I$ recovers iEF \citep{IEF}. Other choices can represent a local quadratic notion of output stability, but the assumptions matter. A positive-semidefinite ambient metric is sufficient only after specifying a tangent space or quotient, a Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse, and the relevant range conditions. Diagonalizing the resulting parameter pullback can further discard cross-parameter geometry.

As one explicit non-Euclidean example, consider probability vectors on an ordered grid with $m$ bins. Let
\[
P=I-\frac{1}{m}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top
\]
project onto the probability-simplex tangent, let $C\in\mathbb R^{(m-1)\times m}$ compute cumulative sums through the penultimate bin, and let $\Delta x$ be the grid spacing. Define
\[
G_{\mathrm{CDF}}=\Delta x\,P C^\top C P.
\]
For a zero-sum probability perturbation $\Delta z$,
\[
\Delta z^\top G_{\mathrm{CDF}}\Delta z
=
\Delta x\,\|C\Delta z\|_2^2,
\]
the squared $L^2$ distance between the corresponding one-dimensional cumulative distribution functions. The matched dual form is
\[
(Pr)^\top G_{\mathrm{CDF}}^+(Pr).
\]
Our implementation tests both identities numerically. This is an ordered-CDF or Cram\'er metric, not sliced Wasserstein. We retain the earlier diffusion experiment only as exploratory evidence that alternative output representations can be inserted into IEWC; it is not used to claim that the diagonal experiment validates Wasserstein geometry.
