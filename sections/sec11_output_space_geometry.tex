\section{Output-Space Geometry}
\label{sec:output-geometry}

The scalar CLOC construction and its OPG matrix require no output-space metric. The level-set construction does: for a symmetric positive-definite primal metric $G_n$, output displacements have inner product $\langle u,v\rangle_{G_n}=u^\top G_n v$, the metric-normal direction is $G_n^{-1}r_n$, and the normal-distance denominator is $r_n^\top G_n^{-1}r_n$. Thus a geometry choice must supply a matched pair: $G_n$ acts on primal output displacement and $G_n^{-1}$ on the loss covector. An arbitrary denominator does not constitute a new geometry.

The canonical choice $G_n=I$ recovers iEF \citep{IEF}. More generally, any smooth output discrepancy whose second-order expansion supplies a valid local quadratic metric can enter IEWC through its matched primal/dual pair $G_n,G_n^{-1}$. For singular ambient metrics, this requires a stated tangent space or quotient, Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse, and range conditions; diagonalizing the parameter pullback can further discard cross-parameter geometry.

For ordered probability outputs, Appendix~\ref{app:ordered-cdf-geometry} constructs a CDF/Cram\'er metric and its matched pseudoinverse dual on the simplex tangent. This preserves the original motivation for space-dependent output geometry but is not sliced Wasserstein. The earlier diffusion experiment therefore remains exploratory evidence for output-representation flexibility rather than validation of a Wasserstein geometry.
