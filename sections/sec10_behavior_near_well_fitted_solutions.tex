\section{Behavior Near Well-Fitted Solutions}

The output-loss-gradient scale in OPG is especially consequential because importance is estimated \emph{after} training on $A$, when many samples may already be well fit. The original EWC Laplace argument likewise expands at an old-task optimum, although its model-Fisher/Hessian motivation is distinct from the observed-label OPG object analyzed here \citep{EWC,KunstnerEF}. Modern expressive models can fit training data up to interpolation \citep{ModernInterp}, so the near-zero regime must be treated explicitly rather than hidden by notation.

To make the comparison precise, consider the per-sample summands
\[
S_n^{\mathrm{OPG}}
=
J_n^\top r_n r_n^\top J_n,
\qquad
S_n^{\mathrm{IEF},G}
=
J_n^\top
\frac{r_n r_n^\top}{r_n^\top G_n^{-1}r_n}
J_n,
\]
where $G_n\succ0$ is the chosen output metric. We assume that $G_n$ is uniformly positive definite and bounded, and that $J_n$ is bounded under the operator norm. More precisely, assume there exists a constant $C_J<\infty$ such that
\[
\|J_n^\top v\|_2
\le
C_J\|v\|_2
\]
for the relevant directions, and constants $0<m_G\le M_G<\infty$ such that
$G_n$ is uniformly bounded between $m_G I$ and $M_G I$.

Using the same $G_n$-normalized direction as above,
\[
\hat r_n^{\,G}
=
\frac{r_n}{\sqrt{r_n^\top G_n^{-1}r_n}},
\qquad
a_n^{\,G}
=
J_n^\top \hat r_n^{\,G},
\qquad
w_n^{\,G}
=
r_n^\top G_n^{-1}r_n,
\qquad
b_n^{\,G}
=
\|J_n^\top \hat r_n^{\,G}\|_2^2,
\]
This gives
\[
S_n^{\mathrm{IEF},G}
=
a_n^{\,G}(a_n^{\,G})^\top,
\qquad
S_n^{\mathrm{OPG}}
=
w_n^{\,G}\,a_n^{\,G}(a_n^{\,G})^\top.
\]

For the spectral norm,
\[
\|S_n^{\mathrm{OPG}}\|_2
=
w_n^{\,G} b_n^{\,G},
\qquad
\|S_n^{\mathrm{IEF},G}\|_2
=
b_n^{\,G}.
\]
Since
\[
\|\hat r_n^{\,G}\|_2^2
=
\frac{\|r_n\|_2^2}{r_n^\top G_n^{-1}r_n},
\]
the metric bounds and bounded-Jacobian assumption give $b_n^{\,G}\in O(1)$, so
\[
\|S_n^{\mathrm{OPG}}\|_2 \in O(w_n^{\,G}),
\qquad
\|S_n^{\mathrm{IEF},G}\|_2 \in O(1).
\]
The sharper lower-bound statement requires a nondegeneracy condition in the normalized output-gradient direction. If there exists $c_J>0$ such that
\[
\|J_n^\top \hat r_n^{\,G}\|_2\ge c_J
\]
for the relevant samples, then
\[
\|S_n^{\mathrm{OPG}}\|_2 \in \Theta(w_n^{\,G}),
\qquad
\|S_n^{\mathrm{IEF},G}\|_2 \in \Theta(1).
\]
We now interpret this for practical situations. Near the end of training on distribution $A$, we expect many old samples to have small gradients. The norm analysis above then shows that OPG contains the additional multiplicative factor $w_n^{\,G}=r_n^\top G_n^{-1}r_n$, whereas undamped IEF removes that factor. Remaining low-magnitude summands of the IEF come from the model sensitivity term $b_n^{\,G}$, not from the scale of the output-loss gradient itself. In the classification setting with $G_n=I$, a confidently correct prediction with $p_{n,c}=1-\varepsilon$ yields
\[
\|r_n\|_2^2=\|p_n-y_n\|_2^2\in\Theta(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Therefore
\[
\|S_n^{\mathrm{OPG}}\|_2 = w_n^{\,I} b_n^{\,I} \in \Theta(\varepsilon^2)\,b_n^{\,I},
\qquad
\|S_n^{\mathrm{IEF},I}\|_2 = b_n^{\,I}.
\]
Under the same nondegeneracy condition on $J_n^\top \hat r_n^{\,I}$, this gives $\|S_n^{\mathrm{OPG}}\|_2 \in \Theta(\varepsilon^2)$ and $\|S_n^{\mathrm{IEF},I}\|_2 \in \Theta(1)$.

The singular case $r_n=0$ remains and must be handled explicitly. As done in \citep{IEF}, one practical solution is to add a damping factor $\tau$ to the quotient, yielding
\[
M_{\mathrm{IEF},G}  
=  
\frac1N  
\sum_{n=1}^N  
\frac{g_n g_n^\top}{r_n^\top G_n^{-1}r_n+\tau},  
\qquad  
g_n=J_n^\top r_n.  
\]
For $s_n=r_n^\top G_n^{-1}r_n>0$, each damped summand admits the exact factorization
\[
S_{n,\tau}^{\mathrm{IEF},G}
=
\frac{g_ng_n^\top}{s_n+\tau}
=
\underbrace{\frac{s_n}{s_n+\tau}}_{\rho_\tau(s_n)}
S_{n,0}^{\mathrm{IEF},G}.
\]
Thus finite damping introduces a transition rather than a uniform scale removal. When $s_n\gg\tau$, $\rho_\tau(s_n)\approx1$ and the undamped direction-normalization analysis applies. When $s_n\ll\tau$, $\rho_\tau(s_n)\approx s_n/\tau$, so small output-loss gradients are again suppressed. Since $g_n=J_n^\top r_n$, fixed positive damping gives $S_{n,\tau}^{\mathrm{IEF},G}\to0$ as $r_n\to0$ under a bounded Jacobian. At exact interpolation, $r_n=g_n=0$; we define the sample contribution as zero. The undamped expression is instead $0/0$ and has no canonical value without specifying a directional limit.

Fixed numerical $\tau$ also has the units of $s_n$. Under a global rescaling of one task loss by $c$, the numerator and $s_n$ scale by $c^2$, but a fixed $\tau$ does not. We therefore use task-relative damping in the confirmatory experiments:
\[
\tau_A
=
\kappa\,\operatorname{median}\{s_i:s_i>0\},
\qquad
\kappa=10^{-2}.
\]
Both $s_n$ and $\tau_A$ then scale by $c^2$, giving
\[
\frac{c^2g_ng_n^\top}{c^2s_n+c^2\tau_A}
=
\frac{g_ng_n^\top}{s_n+\tau_A}.
\]
Task-relative damping therefore restores invariance to global changes of loss units while retaining explicit attenuation near zero gradient. It does not imply invariance to arbitrary sample-dependent monotone transformations under a shared positive damping value. If all recorded $s_i$ are zero, the task importance is defined as the zero matrix.
