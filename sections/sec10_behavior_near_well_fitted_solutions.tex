\section{Behavior Near Well-Fitted Solutions}
\label{sec:well-fitted}

Importance is estimated after training on $A$, when many old samples may be well fit. The original EWC Laplace argument likewise expands at an old-task optimum, although its model-Fisher motivation differs from the observed-label OPG analyzed here \citep{EWC,KunstnerEF}. Modern expressive models can fit training data up to interpolation \citep{ModernInterp}, so the near-zero regime must be explicit. For one sample, let
\[
\begin{aligned}
s_n &= r_n^\top G_n^{-1}r_n,\\
S_n^{\mathrm{OPG}} &= J_n^\top r_nr_n^\top J_n,\\
S_{n,\tau}^{\mathrm{IEF},G}
&= \frac{J_n^\top r_nr_n^\top J_n}{s_n+\tau}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Gradient-scale behavior near a well-fitted solution]
\label{prop:well-fitted-scaling}
Assume $m_GI\preceq G_n\preceq M_GI$ and
$\|J_n^\top v\|_2\le C_J\|v\|_2$. For $s_n>0$,
\[
S_n^{\mathrm{OPG}}
=s_nS_{n,0}^{\mathrm{IEF},G},
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
\|S_n^{\mathrm{OPG}}\|_2&\in O(s_n),\\
\|S_{n,0}^{\mathrm{IEF},G}\|_2&\in O(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
If $\|J_n^\top r_n/\sqrt{s_n}\|_2\ge c_J>0$, these rates are
$\Theta(s_n)$ and $\Theta(1)$. Consequently, for Euclidean softmax
cross-entropy with correct-class probability $1-\varepsilon$, they are
$\Theta(\varepsilon^2)$ and $\Theta(1)$, respectively. For $\tau>0$,
\[
S_{n,\tau}^{\mathrm{IEF},G}
=
\frac{s_n}{s_n+\tau}S_{n,0}^{\mathrm{IEF},G},
\]
so fixed positive damping restores attenuation and
$S_{n,\tau}^{\mathrm{IEF},G}\to0$ as $r_n\to0$. Finally,
\[
\tau_A
=
\kappa\operatorname{median}\{s_i:s_i>0\}
\]
makes every damped summand invariant to a global positive rescaling of the task loss.
\end{proposition}
Appendix~\ref{app:well-fitted-proof} gives the proof.

The proposition isolates the central EWC--IEWC difference. OPG weights each normalized parameter-sensitivity direction by the squared dual norm $s_n$ of the current output-loss gradient; undamped IEF removes that factor, although model sensitivity through $J_n$ remains. Thus a small current loss gradient need not make the corresponding old behavior unimportant for continual learning.

Finite damping introduces a transition rather than uniformly removing gradient scale: normalization applies when $s_n\gg\tau$, while $s_n\ll\tau$ again suppresses the sample. At exact interpolation we define the contribution as zero; the undamped expression is $0/0$ and has no canonical value without a directional limit. We use $\kappa=10^{-2}$ in the confirmatory experiments. Relative damping is invariant only to global loss-unit changes, not arbitrary sample-dependent transformations; if all recorded $s_i$ vanish, the task importance is the zero matrix.
