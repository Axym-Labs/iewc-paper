\section{Conclusion}

We formulated continual learning as preservation of selected functionals of previously learned behavior under subsequent optimization (CLOC) and derived two quadratic relaxations. Any differentiable scalar functional yields a gradient outer-product penalty; choosing the old-sample loss gives OPG, and negative log likelihood gives the empirical Fisher used in many EWC implementations without requiring a model-Fisher interpretation. Our central method, IEWC, instead preserves outputs modulo the tangent to the old loss level set and yields the iEF normalization. This retains the central geometric intuition while locating its exact boundary: undamped normalization removes the samplewise output-loss-gradient scale, whereas positive damping multiplies each summand by $s/(s+\tau)$ and assigns zero contribution at exact interpolation.

Task-relative damping restores invariance to global changes of loss units, and the controlled contamination study directly shows that normalization can prevent high-loss corrupted samples from dominating a full importance matrix in the tested construction. Whether this altered local geometry improves a diagonal continual-learning penalty remains an empirical, domain-conditional question. IEWC is therefore a preservation-first CLOC family: its explicit damping, weighting, output metric, and matrix approximation explain normalized importance geometrically and guide new continual-learning penalties.
