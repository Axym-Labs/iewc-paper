Regularization-based continual learning reduces catastrophic forgetting by
penalizing changes to parameters that mattered for earlier tasks. Elastic Weight
Consolidation (EWC), a standard instance, weights its quadratic penalty by the
empirical Fisher, usually justified as approximating loss curvature. We
instead derive the penalty from what training on a new task should preserve. In
this formulation, \emph{Continual Learning as an Optimization Constraint}
(CLOC), new training must keep chosen functionals of learned behavior unchanged,
and the local approximation of this constraint is the stored penalty. We show
that EWC's empirical Fisher is exactly the first-order form of this constraint
when the preserved functional is each old sample's loss. The derivation exposes
a flaw: EWC weights each sample by the scale of its loss gradient, so samples
the model fits well barely contribute, although later training can still forget
them. Improved EWC (IEWC) instead penalizes how far each old output moves off
its loss level set, rather than how much its loss changes, which removes this
weighting for any differentiable loss and output geometry. Across
classification, segmentation, language, forecasting, and diffusion, IEWC
variants match or improve on EWC and outperform EWC-DR, a classification-specific
correction. In a held-out evaluation with
validation-selected hyperparameters, removing this weighting alone raises ImageNet-R
accuracy over EWC by 2.00 percentage points (95\% CI 0.93--3.07). More
broadly, CLOC turns the design of continual-learning penalties into a choice of
what to preserve, from which a principled penalty follows by derivation.
