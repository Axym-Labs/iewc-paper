Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) is widely used in continual learning, but its importance matrix is implemented in several inequivalent ways. In particular, the observed-label outer product often called the empirical Fisher is neither the model Fisher nor a general curvature matrix. We derive this object instead from old-solution preservation. Starting from continual learning as an optimization constraint (CLOC), we introduce a population, low-order relaxation for constructing scalable local penalties. Preserving each old sample's scalar loss yields an outer product of loss gradients (OPG); for negative log likelihood, this algebraically coincides with the observed-label empirical Fisher used by EWC. Preserving outputs modulo motion tangent to the old loss level set instead yields the improved-empirical-Fisher (iEF) normalization and leads to IEWC. This derivation isolates the mechanism: OPG scales each local sensitivity direction by the squared output-loss-gradient magnitude, whereas undamped iEF removes that scale. We show exactly how positive damping restores attenuation near zero gradient, and introduce task-relative damping that preserves invariance to global loss units. In a controlled contamination study, corrupted samples dominate the OPG trace and rotate its leading directions, while iEF strongly reduces their trace share and remains aligned with the clean-contribution eigenspace. We evaluate the resulting diagonal penalties with locked test sets, paired task orders, matched global scale, and separate uniform and residual-diversity weighting, so that evidence for normalization is not attributed to a weighting heuristic.
