Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) controls forgetting through a quadratic
penalty, but its commonly used observed-label importance matrix lacks a
general-loss derivation and implicitly lets output-loss-gradient magnitudes
determine which samples dominate. We formulate continual learning as preserving
selected properties of an old solution under new-task optimization (CLOC), and
derive scalable penalties through a population, first-order approximation.
Preserving each sample's scalar loss yields an EWC penalty whose importance is
an outer product of loss gradients (OPG) for any differentiable loss; under
negative log likelihood, this is algebraically the observed-label empirical
Fisher conventionally used by EWC, without an appeal to model-Fisher curvature.
Preserving outputs up to first-order motions tangent to the old loss level set
instead yields improved-empirical-Fisher normalization and IEWC. The derivation
determines how output geometry, positive damping, and the sample-aggregation
measure enter the penalty: geometry requires a matched primal/dual metric,
task-relative damping is invariant to global loss units, and explicit weights
replace weighting induced by gradient scale. A controlled contamination
experiment confirms that normalization prevents high-loss corrupted samples
from dominating the full importance matrix. Across classification, language,
forecasting, segmentation, and diffusion, IEWC variants attain strong
continual-learning performance. In an extensive validation-selected locked
study, uniform IEWC improves final average ImageNet-R accuracy over matched EWC
(OPG) by 2.00 percentage points (95\% CI 0.93--3.07) and gives no material mean
difference on TRACE ($-0.11$ points; 95\% CI $-1.48$--$1.27$). These results
support IEWC as a principled refinement of EWC with a domain-dependent empirical
advantage.
